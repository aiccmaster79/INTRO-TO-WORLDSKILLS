Overview

What you actually compete on

A series of practical tasks

There is no written exam. Tasks demonstrate your abilities in PC technology and Cisco networking: installation, configuration, maintenance and troubleshooting of ICT networks, using predominantly Cisco devices.

You also configure hardware and software to meet customer requirements — a client/server network with a domain controller.

Built on Cisco Networking Academy

The competition tests the learning objectives of three Cisco Networking Academy courses:

  • Networking Essentials
  • Linux Unhatched
  • Linux Essentials

The full brief arrives on the day

The complete brief is provided on the morning of the competition. You prepare for the skills, not a specific paper — which is why practising against the clock matters.

Timing: 3 minutes. Stress the last box. Competitors who expect to revise a known task are the ones who freeze. The skill set is published; the specific brief is not.
Structure

Three stages, one pathway

Stage 1 — Entry

A virtual, remote activity that shows both you and WorldSkills UK that you have the competences to proceed. Passing it selects you for stage 2.

Stage 2 — National qualifier

A virtual, remote competition against people across the country. The top 8 scores go through to stage 3.

Stage 3 — National final

An in-person event in Newport, South Wales for 2026. Competitors with the highest marks across all qualifiers are invited.

No automatic entry to the final

Winning a national qualifier does not guarantee a place. Places go to the highest marks across all qualifiers, and WorldSkills UK confirms finalists only after marks have been moderated.

Timing: 3 minutes. The warning box is the most commonly misunderstood rule in the handbook — say it twice. Winning your own qualifier session is not the same as being in the top 8 nationally.

Tooling

The tools change as you progress

Entry & qualifier

  • Cisco Packet Tracer — latest version, for all network build and configuration work
  • Cloud-based virtual machines, with nested VMs
  • Microsoft Hyper-V hosting the virtual machines at qualifier stage
  • Everything delivered remotely, so your own connection and setup matter

National final

  • Real Cisco devices — physical routers and switches on the bench
  • Cisco Modelling Labs (CML) alongside the physical kit
  • Cisco Packet Tracer still in the mix
  • Virtualised PCs and servers for the systems tasks

The jump that catches people out

Simulated kit is forgiving; real kit is not. Console cables, boot times, existing configurations and physical cabling all cost minutes at the final. If you have only ever configured a switch in Packet Tracer, book bench time.

Timing: 4 minutes. If you have real kit in the room, put a switch on the desk now. Ask learners to point out the console port. This is the moment simulation-only learners realise the gap.

Assessment

Marking and assessment

Modules stand alone

Each competition module is assessed and marked independently of every other competition activity. A weak morning does not sink your afternoon.

An industry panel

Judges are selected from a range of industry, college and training provider representatives.

Moderated and assured

Judges' decisions are moderated and quality assured by WorldSkills UK before being confirmed.

Why independent marking helps you

Because modules are marked separately, the right move after a bad task is to draw a line under it and attack the next one. Focus on the marks you can still gain, not the ones you think you lost.

Timing: 3 minutes. This links directly to the judges' "do not worry" tip later. Independent marking is the structural reason that advice actually works.
National final

Four days in November

Shape of the event

  • Day 1 — familiarisation
  • Days 2–3 — competition days
  • Day 4 — grand medal ceremony in the evening

Competitors rotate from task to task in the morning and afternoon of each competition day.

The clock

  • Task duration: 2.5 hours
  • Five hours of competition per day
  • Starting approximately 10:00
  • Finishing approximately 16:00

Each task is distinctly separate from every other task.

Use day 1 properly

Familiarisation is scheduled so you can get acquainted with the tools, materials and equipment you will use. Spend it making sure you are confident in the applications and every piece of equipment — not sightseeing.

Timing: 3 minutes. Emphasise 2.5 hours per task. Most college practicals run an hour or less, so learners consistently underestimate how much can be asked in a single module.

Judges' top tips

What the judges want you to know

Preparation and practice

Be confident, calm and self-assured. Practise your skills and techniques so you get the standard you want with no surprises.

Time management

Work smart, not fast. Tasks have allocated times — practise working to time and under pressure. Running out of time loses marks.

Planning

Make your own plan for each task, work methodically, and write it down to help you prepare.

Organisation

Make sure every stage of the task is completed and all your equipment and materials are organised.

Time constraints

Time yourself, note your mistakes, then repeat — reducing both the time and the number of mistakes. Look for legitimate shortcuts.

Health & safety

Use the appropriate PPE for the task, keep your work area tidy, and work safely.

Clean and tidy

You work more efficiently in a tidy area, and your work should be clean and ready when you present it for judging.

Understanding

Read the brief thoroughly. Do not be afraid to ask — the only silly question is the one you do not ask.

Do not worry, and enjoy it

If a task goes badly, draw a line under it and move on. Getting to a qualifier or final is a massive achievement — make the most of it.

Timing: 5 minutes. Rather than reading all nine, ask learners which two they personally most need. Time management and planning almost always win, which sets up the practical task later.

Case study

Case study: a realistic route to Newport

Where Amara started

A second-year IT apprentice, comfortable rebuilding laptops and resetting passwords. She had finished Networking Essentials but had never configured a router outside a worksheet, and had never used Linux.

Her first honest readiness audit scored 4 of 12.

What she changed

  • Finished Linux Unhatched, then Linux Essentials
  • Built the same client/server lab five times, timing each run
  • Practised subnetting on paper daily until it was automatic
  • Asked her tutor for two hours a week on real Cisco kit
  • Wrote a plan before every practice task

The outcome that matters

Amara placed third in her qualifier session and still reached the final — places go to the highest marks across all qualifiers. The habit that moved her score most was planning before configuring.

Timing: 5 minutes. This scenario is an illustrative composite for classroom use, not a named competitor — say so if asked. The takeaway is that consistency and timed repetition beat last-minute cramming.
Rules

Competition rules

Completing your registration means you have agreed to the WorldSkills UK Competition Rules. Make sure you are familiar with them before any competition activity. On top of those, this competition adds its own:

Not permitted

  • Mobile phones on during competition activity — they must be switched off
  • Listening to music through headphones
  • Communicating with other competitors during competition activity
  • Extra time because you arrived late for a session

Expected of you

  • Address any question during competition activity to the competition judge
  • Arrive on time for each session — that responsibility is yours
  • Report technical failure of your equipment to your judge immediately
  • Accept that extra time is allocated only when the fault is beyond your control
Timing: 3 minutes. The two easiest rules to break by accident are the phone one (silent is not off) and the headphones one. The next slide tests exactly these.

Practical task: build your training plan

Brief

Using your readiness audit score, write a personal training plan that takes you from where you are now to the standard the qualifier demands.

  • Name your weakest of the four final modules and give it the most time
  • Map each unticked audit item to a specific practice activity
  • Include the Cisco courses you still need
  • Schedule at least one timed 2.5-hour run of a full task
  • Say how you will get access to real Cisco kit
  • Write down what you will do in the first 15 minutes of any task

Evidence checklist

  • Your audit percentage and the weakest area it named
  • A written plan with dated, specific activities
  • One timed task with a note of where the time went
  • A second audit score, retaken after four practice sessions
  • Your rules-judge score and any scenario you got wrong
  • A reflection: what will you ask your tutor for?
Timing: 12 minutes for the briefing plus independent work. Insist the plan is dated and specific. "Get better at subnetting" is not a plan; "20 subnetting questions every morning before class" is.

Jargon buster

No terms match that search.

Active DirectoryMicrosoft's directory service holding users, groups and computers, and the thing a domain controller runs.
Domain controllerA server that hosts Active Directory and authenticates users on the domain.
Client/server networkA network where central servers provide services and storage to client machines, rather than peer-to-peer sharing.
Group policyCentrally managed settings pushed to users and computers; a GPO is linked to a site, domain or OU.
Software RAIDCombining disks for redundancy or performance using the operating system rather than a hardware controller.
File and folder securityPermissions controlling who can read, change or own data — usually NTFS permissions combined with share permissions.
Remote accessLetting an authorised user reach a system across the network, for example by remote desktop or VPN.
DHCPDynamic Host Configuration Protocol: hands out IP addresses and options to clients automatically.
DNSDomain Name System: resolves names such as server01.college.local to IP addresses.
SubnettingDividing an IP address range into smaller networks by moving the boundary between network and host bits.
IPv6The 128-bit addressing scheme that succeeds IPv4; in scope at every stage of this competition.
Static routingManually configured routes, including the default route used when nothing more specific matches.
OSPFOpen Shortest Path First: a link-state dynamic routing protocol that learns routes by exchanging updates with neighbours.
EIGRPEnhanced Interior Gateway Routing Protocol: Cisco's advanced distance-vector routing protocol.
NAT / PATNetwork and Port Address Translation: rewrites addresses so many private hosts can share public addressing.
VLANA logically separate network on shared switch hardware, each one its own broadcast domain.
Inter-VLAN routingRouting traffic between VLANs, using a router subinterface or a layer 3 switch interface.
EtherChannelBundling several physical links into one logical link for more bandwidth and resilience.
Device hardeningSecuring and managing network devices — strong credentials, secure management access, unused services disabled.
Packet TracerCisco's network simulator, used at entry and qualifier stages and still present at the final.
Cisco Modelling LabsCisco's network modelling and virtualisation platform, used at the national final alongside real devices.
Hyper-VMicrosoft's hypervisor, used to host the virtual machines — including nested VMs at entry and qualifier stage.
Straight-through / crossoverThe two Ethernet cable wiring patterns; you are expected to know which one a given connection needs.
FamiliarisationScheduled time at the final to get acquainted with the tools, materials and equipment before competing.
ModerationThe quality-assurance step where WorldSkills UK reviews judges' marks before results are confirmed.
Champions programmeContinued involvement for competitors past the finals, including inspiring learners at schools and colleges.

Four things to remember

Three stages, one standard

Entry, qualifier and final test the same skill set at rising realism. Top 8 marks nationally reach the final — winning your own session is not enough.

Two halves, equal weight

Networking and Windows systems both carry marks. Neglecting Active Directory, RAID or Linux costs you as much as weak routing does.

The clock is a skill

2.5 hours per task. Plan first, set a halfway checkpoint, and practise timed runs until finishing is normal.

Simulated is not real

Packet Tracer gets you through the remote stages. Real Cisco kit and CML decide the final, so get bench time early.

The job this competition points at
